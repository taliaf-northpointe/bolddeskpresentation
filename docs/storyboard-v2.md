# The Next Big Idea — Nora Presents BoldDesk

**A visual storyboard and production guide · Revision 2**

## From managing inboxes to managing experiences

A leadership-focused story about faster support, stronger teamwork, and a better customer experience.

- **Recommended runtime:** 5:00 – 5:15 as written (see the runtime note below)
- **Primary build:** Vyond | **Nora clips:** Hedra | **Format:** 16:9, 1080p

## Runtime note — read this first

The previous draft targeted 3:45 to 4:15, but its narration totalled roughly 625 words. At a natural presenter pace of about 140 words per minute that is 4:28 of speech before any holds, pauses, or the two-second end title. Two scenes were badly over-packed: Scene 12 ran at roughly 227 words per minute and Scene 9 at about 183. Neither is speakable.

Every scene in this revision is timed at 130 to 145 words per minute, which is why the total is now honest at just over five minutes. That is long for a contest submission, so here is the trim path in the order I would cut:

1. Leave out Scene 7A. It is optional and aimed at a different audience.
2. Cut Scene 7 (thirty seconds). Its point is implied by Scene 5 and it is the least load-bearing feature scene. → about 4:50
3. Cut Scene 11 (room to grow). Losing it drops the AI angle, so keep it if the AI or emerging technology category matters to the submission. → about 4:33
4. Cut Scene 10 (automation). This is the last one I would give up, since it is the proof that the platform extends to what Northpointe already uses. → about 4:08

If a hard ceiling of four minutes applies, the strongest possible cut is Scenes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12 — the full emotional arc plus the portal, the leadership question, and the close, with every pure feature scene removed. That lands at roughly 3:55.

## Creative direction

The video should feel like a short animated business story, not a product walkthrough. Nora is the warm guide, while the animation proves the impact through familiar moments: a crowded shared inbox, an employee waiting on help, a borrower waiting on that employee, and a support team finally gaining shared visibility.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Core message** | Better internal support helps employees serve borrowers, customers, and business partners faster. |
| **Emotional arc** | Friction and waiting → clarity and momentum → shared purpose. |
| **Nora's role** | Open the idea, bridge between sections, deliver the biggest message, and close. Avoid keeping her on screen continuously. |
| **Visual style** | Friendly 2D corporate animation with clean shapes, natural movement, and a restrained Northpointe-inspired palette. |
| **Framing** | BoldDesk is presented as something being trialed, not a decision already made. Vendor management review is still open and the platform has not been announced company-wide. |
| **What to avoid** | Do not name specific teams or departments as being in BoldDesk. Do not imply IT is on the platform. Do not cite delivery progress or issue counts. |

## Production rules

- Use Nora on camera for roughly 30 to 35 percent of the runtime. Let the rest play as animated examples with her voice continuing underneath.
- Keep on-screen copy short. Never display the full narration as text. Use one strong phrase or proof point at a time.
- Make the employee-to-borrower moment the visual centerpiece. Hold on the waiting customer long enough for leadership to feel the consequence.
- Use real BoldDesk screenshots only where they add credibility. Rebuild or crop them cleanly so small interface text does not become unreadable.
- Keep humor light. Nora can be warm and personable, but the customer impact remains the center of the pitch.
- Keep every example generic. Personas are roles, not named teams.

## Video rhythm

Open human → show the friction → connect it to the customer → reveal the better experience → prove operational value → close on shared purpose.

## Production notes on Nora

- Hedra's Character-3 model outputs at 720p, while the rest of this video is 1080p. Cutting straight from crisp Vyond vector art to a softer 720p clip will show. Test one cut before building eleven scenes on the assumption — then either upscale the Hedra clips, frame Nora smaller in the composition so the softness is less apparent, or accept it.
- Hedra has no transparent-background output. Build the setting into the still image you feed it, or generate Nora against a flat solid colour and key that out in Vyond.
- Generate the full voice track as one continuous file before splitting it. Segment-by-segment generation causes tonal drift that is audible at every cut.

## Suggested build order

1. Record or generate the final Nora voice track first, as one file. Use the same voice for narration over the animated scenes.
2. Build Scenes 2 and 3 first. They establish the email problem and the customer consequence, and they set the visual standard for everything after.
3. Build the recurring ticket, email, and character assets once, then reuse them for continuity.
4. Add Nora clips after scene timing is stable. This prevents lip-sync clips from controlling the pace of the whole edit.
5. Add title cards, sound effects, music, and the final logo only after the story works without them.
6. Export a review version with captions, then watch once with the sound off to confirm the story still reads visually.
7. Time the rough cut against the runtime note above and make the trim decision before polishing anything.
